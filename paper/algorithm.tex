\subsection{Action selection on a shared observation}
At each control query, a frozen SmolVLA actor samples four candidate action
chunks using independent noise. The released VGAS critic ranks these chunks.
For each candidate it uses the lower of its two value estimates; the executor
dispatches the highest-ranked chunk. Our implementation encodes the observation
once per query, shares its attention cache across the four denoising branches,
and passes its features to the critic. Cross-candidate prefix reuse has
precedents in language-model inference and VLA systems, including Zora and
PACT-VLA. Our contributions are the integration for a flow actor and learned
critic, the system-level timing measurements, and the evaluation of per-channel
INT8 storage for the frozen critic.

\subsection{Closed-loop and hardware evaluation}
The actor generates 50 steps, the Object critic scores the first 32 and the
Long critic scores all 50. The executor commits up to 20 actions before a new
observation. The single-candidate control and the original four-candidate
implementation respectively isolate the task effect of selection and the
systems effect of observation reuse. Object covers ten tasks; Long covers
Task2, selected during development, with two noise repeats per physical layout.
Both suites use the same LIBERO simulator. Fixed lighting variants provide a
separate perturbation check. GPU measurements use an A100; deployment latency,
energy and bandwidth on the intended chip remain open.

On Object, the critic matrices are also stored as signed eight-bit integers
with per-channel scales and unpacked to the original arithmetic precision on
access. Reported bytes count persistent critic tensors including scales.
The paired success interval describes uncertainty in the observed difference;
formal equivalence requires a prespecified margin and an equivalence test.

\subsection{Replay of the ten-step denoising loop}
On a fixed Object observation, we captured the ten action-expert iterations
with CUDA Graphs and refreshed the prefix cache and noise for every replay.
At four candidates, warm replay reduced the median expert time from 189.84
to 47.38 ms and full decision time from 225.74 to 80.54 ms on A100.
After capture, raising the candidate count from one to sixteen increased
full decision latency from 69.22 to 119.78 ms. The weak dependence on batch
size observed in eager execution therefore depends on its scheduling regime.
Measurements exclude one-time capture. On nine strict FP32 comparisons,
the returned candidate actions, critic scores and selected indices agreed.
Closed-loop success was measured for the eager implementation.
